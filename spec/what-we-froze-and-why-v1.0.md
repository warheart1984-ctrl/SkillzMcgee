## 1. Why We Froze Version 1.0

We froze Version 1.0 because the architecture reached a point where:

- semantics were coherent
- invariants were complete
- governance was defined
- conformance was testable
- modeling was unified
- operators could inspect constitutional state
- drift could be classified
- lineage could be reconstructed

At that moment, further expansion would have weakened the foundation rather than strengthened it.

---

## 2. What We Froze

We froze:

- CAR-1.0
- constitutional invariants
- semantic grammar
- proof graph semantics
- evidence and provenance model
- constitutional loop
- compatibility rules
- stability principle
- amendment procedure
- governance charter
- stewardship mandate

These define the identity of the platform.

---

## 3. What We Did Not Freeze

We intentionally left unfrozen:

- implementations
- tooling
- operator workflows
- modeling tools
- dashboards
- visualizers
- documentation
- public messaging

These can evolve freely as long as semantics remain intact.

---

## 4. Why This Matters

By freezing semantics and freeing implementation, we created:

- a stable constitutional platform
- a durable foundation for future generations
- a system that can evolve without losing identity
- a governance model that prevents drift
- a compatibility promise that survives implementations

This is how Continuity OS becomes more than a project.
It becomes a constitutional machine.
